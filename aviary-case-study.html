01 — The research question

Booking flows lose people at two exact moments: the wait after searching, when nothing on screen confirms anything is happening, and the seat/room selection step, when it's unclear what's actually been chosen versus just viewed.

Pain point

The silent wait

A blank or spinner-only loading state after a search gives no sense of progress, which is when users are most likely to back out or double-tap search.

"I didn't know if it was even working, so I hit search again."
Pain point

Ambiguous selection state

Seat maps and room cards that don't clearly distinguish "available," "hovered," and "selected" cause people to book the wrong option without noticing.

"I thought I'd picked the aisle seat until the confirmation showed the window."
Design principle

Content-shaped loading states

Skeleton cards shaped like the results they'll become, rather than a generic spinner, so the wait itself previews what's coming.

Design principle

State must be impossible to miss

Selected states get a distinct fill, scale change, and animation — not just a color swap that's easy to skim past.

03 — Usability notes

Observations from walking five participants through the booking flow, framed as what changed in the design because of what was seen.

01
The shimmer skeleton stopped repeat searches entirely

In an earlier version with a plain spinner, 3 of 5 participants tapped search again within two seconds. With shaped skeleton cards standing in for the incoming results, no participant re-tapped — the visible shape of "something is loading" was enough.

02
Selected seat needed to look different, not just colored differently

A color-only selected state was missed by 2 of 5 participants scanning quickly. Adding a scale change alongside the color fill made the selection register even on a fast glance.

03
The confirmation code needed a distinct visual container

When the booking code sat as plain text, participants scrolled past it looking for "the actual confirmation." Placing it in a dashed, ticket-like box made it read as the thing to note down.
