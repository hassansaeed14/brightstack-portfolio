01 — The research question

Before any screen was drawn, the brief was narrowed to one problem: first-time investors don't avoid investing apps because the apps are ugly — they avoid them because the apps assume knowledge they don't have yet.

Pain point

Jargon as a wall

Terms like "expense ratio" and "diversification" are treated as given, not explained — so a new user either guesses or gives up before funding an account.

"I didn't know if I was buying one thing or fifty things."
Pain point

Fear of an irreversible mistake

Confirmation screens for existing apps read like legal disclaimers, which makes a small, reversible first deposit feel bigger and riskier than it is.

"It felt like signing something, not trying something."
Design principle

Plain language over financial vocabulary

Every label is written the way a knowledgeable friend would say it out loud, with the technical term available on tap, never required to proceed.

Design principle

Small, reversible first step

The first investment is framed explicitly as adjustable — a slider with a visible floor, not a fixed commitment — to lower the stakes of starting.

04 — Usability notes

Observations from walking five first-time-investor participants through the flow, framed as what changed in the design because of what was seen.

01
The word "confirm" alone still read as final

Adding the "reversible anytime" badge above the confirmation copy reduced hesitation at that screen — participants tapped through noticeably faster once the reversibility was stated up front rather than implied.

02
Fund names needed a plain-language subtitle

"Starter Growth Fund" alone prompted a pause; adding "a mix of 40 large companies" beneath it let participants make a choice without leaving the screen to look the term up.

03
A slider lowered the perceived stakes of the first deposit

Compared to a numeric input tested earlier in the process, the slider with a visible floor made participants more willing to start below the amount they said they'd "typically" invest — treating it as a trial rather than a commitment.
